# Dry cleaning & miscellaneous

Inventory → Dry cleaning & miscellaneous logs multi-SKU batches (up to 50 SKU/location lines), cleaner names, expected returns, condition reasons and notes. The register tracks partial returns, inspection, cleaning-to-not-for-sale reclassification, disposal, overdue batches, named actors and a permanent action history.

Stock quantity source is now Shopify, not the saved physical-count JSON. Available is used by sales, stock search and replenishment. Total owned uses Shopify `on_hand`, which includes available, committed and unavailable states at every location. Display and Warehouse follow the existing showroom location mapping; other locations remain included in overall totals. Existing physical-count classifications and rack labels are retained as reference, clearly labeled. Stock movements can use live location quantities with staff-confirmed racks; audited per-rack balances still require a reviewed baseline.

Cleaning moves Shopify `available` to `quality_control`. Miscellaneous moves `available` to `damaged` (the register records the more specific reason, including altered returns). Ready-for-sale returns move the original state back to `available` at the original location. Cleaning reclassification moves `quality_control` to `damaged`. Disposal reduces `damaged` and `on_hand`; it never restores availability. Disposal history is an inventory record, not an automatic accounting expense. Other Shopify inspections and reservations are shown separately, not counted as cleaning batches.

Inventory/warehouse staff may request batches. Owner/admin, existing `STOCK_MOVEMENT_APPROVERS`, and the named inventory lead `tushar` (while holding Inventory or Warehouse access) may confirm batches, inspect returns, reclassify, or dispose. Their authority is shared with stock movements and allows reviewing their own or other employees' requests. Manager-created batches confirm immediately. Staff must keep pieces in place until the manager confirms the Shopify change. Untracked items, duplicate SKUs, products permitting overselling, unknown locations, excessive quantities, and SKUs with unresolved rack movements are rejected.

`inventory_care.json` lives beside `DATA_PATH` on the persistent volume (override with `INVENTORY_CARE_PATH`). Atomic writes preserve the batch and exact Shopify mutation before dispatch. Shopify state moves use compare-and-swap on both quantities and a persisted idempotency key. Unknown results stay sync-pending and block related operations; retry uses the exact same mutation. Confirmed actions cannot run twice. Definitively rejected actions may be cancelled with a reason and submitted again after inspection. An unknown dispatch cannot be cancelled. After 23 hours an uncertain action requires inventory-manager reconciliation against Shopify history, rather than retrying an expired idempotency key.

Snapshot reads paginate all items and all item locations; incomplete pages or quantities fail explicitly. The shared snapshot expires after 60 seconds and confirmed care operations invalidate it immediately. Dashboard and care display reads retain the last confirmed snapshot while Shopify refreshes in the background, with its timestamp and any refresh error visible. The derived `inventory-snapshot.json` cache lives beside `DATA_PATH` (override with `INVENTORY_SNAPSHOT_PATH`) so a restart can display the previous successful update. A stale display snapshot uses care balances as of that update. It never authorizes a write or replaces Shopify stock with the static count. Care confirmations read the specific SKU or inventory-item IDs fresh and retain all quantity, tracking, mapping, compare-and-swap and idempotency checks.

Care pending requests, outstanding batches and history use `?registerOnly=1`, independently of Shopify reads. Quantities use `?quantitiesOnly=1`; a quantity response never replaces register status. The legacy combined response reads the register after awaiting Shopify so a concurrent cancellation stays cancelled. Clients poll background refresh status and use `knownAt` to avoid repeatedly downloading unchanged quantities. The dashboard also updates every minute and receives a refresh signal from care changes in another tab.

The inventory catalogue displays 50 products per page. Search, filters and quantity totals cover the complete catalogue; pagination only limits rendered rows. Saved physical-count classifications remain the first reference. Missing values come from explicit Shopify `custom` metafields, SANKI classification tags, then posted purchasing records matched by SKU. Known category aliases are normalized. Missing or conflicting classifications are marked for review without writing metadata, costs or quantities back to Shopify or altering purchase records. Rack references remain unchanged.

When rack movements are activated, outstanding care and disposed quantities are deducted from that rack's available position. Returns restore the original position; use the existing move workflow afterward to transfer racks/locations. Care writes and rack movement writes share one serial queue. Do not import a new movement opening baseline that already excludes care quantities without reconciling outstanding care records first. Without an approved baseline, movements use live Shopify available quantities, retain manager review, and record staff-confirmed rack labels without inventing rack balances.

Examples: 10,000 owned → 50 cleaning + 10 not for sale = 9,940 available, 10,000 owned. Returning 48 clean pieces and reclassifying 2 damaged pieces gives 9,988 available, 0 cleaning, 12 not for sale, 10,000 owned. Disposing 2 gives 9,998 owned; simply setting pieces aside does not change owned stock.

Potential later additions: cleaner bills and cost per batch linked to expenses, condition photos, vendor handover/return slips, supplier claims, repair tracking, sample loans for shoots, and return inspection intake. Customer reservations should use Shopify draft orders so commitments stay in its order workflow.
